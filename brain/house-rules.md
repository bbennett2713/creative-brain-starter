> Starter rules from Smart Marketer. Keep them, cut them, or argue with them.

## Starter rules

1. **Judge an ad by its job, not pass/fail.**
   Some ads grab attention, some explain, some close the sale. Compare each one to ads doing the same job.
2. **The ad that gets the spend is the proven one.**
   Meta puts money behind what works. A small test with a great return is a candidate, not a winner yet.
3. **One ad carrying most of the spend is normal.**
   That's the system finding a winner. Feed it new versions instead of forcing money elsewhere.
4. **The words and the picture tell you more than the category.**
   "UGC" or "testimonial" says little. The actual headline, hook and image tell you why it worked.
5. **The biggest opportunity is what you haven't tried.**
   When deciding what's next, look for missing angles and formats, not just more of what you have.
6. **A weak angle is usually underbuilt, not disproven.**
   One tired ad doesn't kill an idea. Give it a better headline and photo before you drop it.
7. **Never conclude from one test.**
   One result is a hint. It becomes a rule when it happens again.
8. **50 near-identical ads teach you one thing 50 times.**
   Volume only helps when the ads are actually different. Change the angle, not just the color.
9. **Never let AI draw your product.**
   People notice a wrong label or a warped shape, and it costs you their trust. Use your real photos, cropped.
10. **Never say the same correction twice.**
    If you've said it once, it's a rule now. Having to repeat yourself means the system failed, not you.
11. **Borrow layouts from your own category.**
    What works for a luxury bag won't sell grout cleaner. Your references should look like your world and your price.
12. **The format is part of the argument.**
    A Q&A says "you're wondering this". A before/after says "look at the difference". A phone-caption post says "a real person, not an ad". Pick the format for what it argues, not for variety.
13. **People buy for functional and emotional reasons.**
    It works, it's faster, it fits (functional). How it makes them feel, who it makes them (emotional). Know which one each ad leads with, and don't forget the one you usually skip.

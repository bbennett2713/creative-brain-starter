# Checkpoint 8: Second semester

**The idea.** The brain leaves theory and starts learning from its own work. It reads the results of the test it planned, diagnoses what happened, and designs the next experiment. Each loop it needs less supervision, and starts saving time instead of taking it.

A batch isn't finished until it's been read.

---

1. **Get the results.** A new ad-level export in `results/` covering the test's dates (same columns as checkpoint 2). Run the same file checks: real date range, rows with the same ad name added together, the purchase minimum before judging, promo and catalog ads flagged.
2. **Ask before you reveal:** "Before I show you the numbers: what do you think happened?" Then remind them of their prediction from `plan.md`.
3. **Read the test against its own success measure,** not against a general "did it do well". Same guardrails as checkpoint 2: spend is the signal, judge each ad by its job, no budget advice, one test is a hint.
   - Did the test actually test what it meant to? (Enough spend per ad? Launched together? Did the ads differ only in what the plan varied?) If not, say so plainly: that's a lesson about the test, not the idea.
   - What won, what lost, and **why**, looking at what the ads actually said and showed.
   - Did anything surprise you? What would the experts on the reading list say?
4. **Write the result** into the batch's `plan.md` under `Result:`: what happened, the evidence, the prediction vs the result, and what we now believe.
5. **Update the brain:**
   - `brain/what-worked.md`: winners and why, with the evidence.
   - `brain/account-story.md`: the story has changed; say how.
   - `brain/roadmap.md`: move the lane (keep going, change it, or close it), following the plan's "if it wins / if it loses".
   - Any hypothesis confirmed more than once can become a rule in `brain/house-rules.md`. Ask the founder first: "Yes, that's true now?"
6. **Draft the next plan card** (back to checkpoint 6) and let the founder react.

- **Hand back:** "What we learned in three lines, and what I'd test next."

**Done when:** the batch's `plan.md` has a `Result:` section. Then back to checkpoint 6.

**Over time:** the interview gets shorter, the brain notices things before the founder does, and the founder's job becomes judging, not explaining. That's graduation: regular reports, the experts' news, analysis they wouldn't have had time for, the next batch planned from evidence, a base layer of ads for volume, and the learning from every test. It still never decides what's true. They do.
